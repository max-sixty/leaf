Threads / implementation review

Less space between threads. More room to read.

The real Threads panel now uses the compact accordion. Open Threads in the banner to try it. If all discussions are resolved, choose Filters → Resolved. The sketch below records the chosen design and spacing alternatives.

Try the real Threads panel

One conversation expands at a time. The reply field spans the conversation’s width. Author and time sit above each message; delivery receipts sit below the message they describe. Unfinished drafts survive switching threads. Search, filters, and links to individual messages reveal their target.

Use Enter or Space on a title to expand it; arrow keys and Home/End move between titles. Use t / Shift+t to walk threads, c to reply, and Cmd/Ctrl+Enter to send. Escape leaves the reply box, collapses the conversation, then steps back out of the panel.

Sending and Sent share a delivery line so confirmation does not shift the conversation. The local review also checks that collapsed titles retain reader obligations and ongoing agent work. The richer activity ontology remains separate work.

Keep the next turn visible

Collapsed titles show On you when the reader owes the next turn. Ongoing agent work stays visible even when a newer message is queued. Full delivery details remain with their message.

Keep the structure, remove the repetition

The title and message count remain compact. An open conversation uses one shared inset for its quote, messages, and reply field. Author and time form a small header instead of consuming a separate column. A Draft marker preserves unfinished work, and the bottom control collapses long threads.

Carbon’s accordion guidance explicitly allows inputs to fill the panel even when prose has different margins. That supports the full-width composer here; precise spacing remains a Leaf design choice.

Signal distinguishes Sent, Delivered, and Read for each message. Applying that distinction here, Sent belongs beneath the sent message and no longer appears as a thread title’s status. Current work and failure states can still help identify a thread before opening it.

Component reuse is possible: Web Awesome supplies compatible web components, while Radix uses React and would introduce another rendering framework beside Leaf’s Lit components. Neither decides chat spacing or message delivery placement. This pass keeps Leaf’s components; a broader disclosure refactor could use native details/summary, including exclusive groups, without adding a library.
