Summary

The only paragraph most customers will read, and it goes out in your voice: double-click it (or its ✎) and rewrite it. 

On the morning of 12 June, exports were delayed for 94 minutes. Nothing was lost: anything you exported in that window arrived late, but it arrived. One stuck job held up the queue, and the fault was in how we retry failures. We've changed how that works; the details are below. We're sorry for the delay.

There is nothing you need to do. Every delayed export was delivered by 10:48 UTC; if something downstream of you noticed the gap, reply on your support thread and we'll pull the delivery log for your workspace.

What happened

Exports run through a queue. At 09:14 our monitoring flagged it backing up, and by 10:48 everything delayed had been delivered. The fault was in how we retry a failing export.

The failing job re-entered the queue at its head position on each retry attempt, serialized ahead of all subsequent export jobs for the duration of the incident window.

Each retry put the failing export back at the front of the line, and every export behind it waited — the longest for 81 minutes — on a job that was never going to finish.

The trigger was a workspace containing malformed attachment metadata that our systems did not expect.

I'm proposing the deletion because the sentence points at a customer's data, and the fault wasn't theirs: our validator accepted the attachment at upload, and our worker retried a job that could never succeed. The paragraph before it already says everything a reader needs.

The mechanism below stays internal. The worker treats every failure as retryable, so a job that can never succeed comes back to the head of the queue every 45 seconds.

A declared attachment size of −1 fails serialization every time

The upload predates the size check added in March, and two other workspaces carry one; both are quarantined now. This is what the sentence proposed for deletion was pointing at, and it reads as blame however it is worded.

What we've changed

  1. We no longer retry a job that cannot succeed; we set it aside and page a person.
  2. A retried job now rejoins the queue at the back, so no single export can hold up the rest again. Both changes shipped on 13 June.
  3. We are adding an alert on any one job failing repeatedly — on 12 June, nothing fired until the queue behind the stuck job was long enough to notice.

This writeup is the record of the incident, so when the alerting change ships we will note it here rather than in a new post.
