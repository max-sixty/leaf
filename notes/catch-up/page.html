Design · catch-up

Where should catch-up show each item?

Catch-up is one pass through On me: the items come one at a time, each with its own answers and a Later, and the pass ends on what was answered and what was put off. The swiper did this for one kind of card and was retired for ordinary choices in #2157; a pass over On me brings its pace back for every kind of decision. It needs no new widget, event or per-widget code, because every item on On me can already be answered away from the page. What's open is where the item is shown while the user answers it.

Every kind of item already has its answers

Each widget declares its answering controls as Decision commands (decision: true in its command rows). The On view already turns those into buttons on each card, pressing the widget's own control, so an answer there is recorded exactly as a press on the page. Catch-up reads the same list, readQueues().on_user, in the order m walks it, arrives at each item the way m does, and counts an item answered when the server's reading drops it from On me. A widget added later gets catch-up through its declarations alone.

Later records nothing. The item stays on On me, and the pass's last screen offers to go through the ones put off, and only those. Slack's "keep unread" has nothing to undo here, since an obligation leaves On me only when it is answered. An item that reaches On me during a pass joins its end.

The pass starts from Catch up in the On view. Within a pass, m steps on (Later on an open item, Next on an answered one), Shift+M steps back and Escape ends it, so the keys the user already walks On me with also walk the pass. On a touch screen, a sideways swipe on the card or bar does the same. The prototypes have all three.

Which catch-up should we build?

Both prototypes run on today's runtime over the same page; their bar and card are page scripts standing where the real one would join Leaf's panel. Press Catch up in either, answer a few items, put some off with Later, and finish the pass; each sample's Full view button gives it the whole window. I recommend the card. For an item answered with buttons, its answers stay in one place from item to item while the page shows the item's evidence behind it, and on a phone the user answers with a thumb without reaching up into the page. It costs a second copy of those buttons beside the widget's own, as the On view already has, and gains nothing for a draft or a question, which are answered on the page or in a thread with either candidate.

With "Answering moves to the next item" on, the next item's answers take the place of the ones just pressed, so a quick second press answers an item the user hasn't read. The On view keeps an answered card where it stood for the same reason, so I recommend leaving it off: the user presses Next where Later stood.

The page is the card. Each item arrives where it sits and the rest of the page dims; the user answers with the widget itself. The bar holds the count, Later and Next.

One On card at a time, its answers in the same place each time; the page travels to the item behind it. With travel off, the page stays put and Show goes to the item.

No swipe widget: the pass works over ordinary decisions

The swipe deck, retired in #2157, sorted its own cards into pass and keep piles and nothing else. A pass over On me gives every decision the same pace, so an agent that wants a quick triage writes one lf-options per proposal, as authoring-decisions.md already asks, and the user catches up through them. An agent that wants a queue worked one item at a time on the page already has one: lf-tabs list="side" with lf-tally next. A later step could let the tally's Next on me start a pass over its region alone (obligationsIn), which needs no new widget either.

Should catch-up also walk unread replies?

Slack's catch-up walks unread messages. In Leaf those are agent replies in threads, and the research behind On me found they are most of what the user leaves waiting: results they never acknowledged, more than questions they never answered (notes/what-needs-you/research.md). A thread's words count as read once they have been on screen, through a read event, so a Keep unread would have to put them back: a new event kind and a protocol bump.

What building it touches

The browser alone: the pass lives in this tab and reads the On list, the walk and the Decision commands that exist today. The panel (floating-panel.js) gains the Catch up entry in the On view and the bar or card; the walk (queue-walk.js) gives the pass its order and arrival; the card reuses the On view's card and choicesOf. The server, the protocol and the widgets stay as they are. Agents need no new instructions: authoring-decisions.md already asks for each decision to be answerable where On me lands, which is where catch-up shows it.
