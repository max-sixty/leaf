Using a page

Using a Leaf page

An agent hands the user a page about its work. The user reads it, comments on exact passages, makes the choices it asks for, and follows what the agent is doing, by keyboard, pointer or touch.

This guide is for the person working on a page: the floating panel, threads, On Me, and the keys and touch routes. How it works explains the mechanism behind it, and Extending covers writing pages and packages. Leaf is experimental software. Browser support gaps lists what is known to differ in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari and their mobile versions.

Panel, rail and thread cards

Leaf adds controls around the authored HTML. This interface is called chrome: the Leaf panel floating at the window's foot, comment and decision markers, and thread cards. At rest the panel is one row: the leaf, coloured by the agent's state; the comment field, which shows the agent's status while it is empty; and the On Me mark, counting what waits on the user, with a dot while a thread holds a reply they haven't read. The leaf opens a small menu of the page's other controls. Pressing the On Me mark opens its view above the row. A ✕ replaces the mark while the view is open and closes it when pressed. A folded panel shows only the leaf in the window's corner.

The On view shows one list at a time, under a tab named for its actor, with completed items under Done, and a Threads tab listing the open threads under Open, then the resolved ones under Resolved. On Me, below, says what the lists hold and how an item is answered. Opening a view preserves the page’s text wrapping and reading position.

The page reserves space at the bottom for the panel's row, so the last lines can scroll above it. A view opens above the row and overlays the page without moving its content. Escape or a press on the page closes the view. Phones and touch screens describes the panel on a touch screen.

The rail is the strip to the right of main where Leaf places comment and decision markers. It appears when the window has enough space: about 960px on a column page or about 1920px on a sidebar page. Where no rail fits, and inside a scrolling pane, a small pin appears beside the target on the page. Leaf places it clear of text and controls when space permits. The o shortcut hides pins and passage highlights.

A thread card opens beside its marker in the margin. If the margin is narrower than the card, it overlaps the text's edge. Threads, a thread opened from it, the On view and All pages open as views above the panel's row, one at a time.

What the panel's line says

The panel's comment field, while empty, is Leaf's one line of words about the page. It says one thing at a time, the first of these that applies:

Reading Example
An error about no particular box, until the user next types Couldn't load v3
Where words go, while the caret is in the field Comment on the page, Reply in this thread
Words selected on the page, which a press on the field or c comments on “the queue fills”
A notice, or a walk's position while the user stays on its stop Updated to v4, Thread 2 of 5
The mode the user is in, which the ✕ at the row's end leaves In Draw mode
The page's status running the browser suite

With a thread open, the field is that thread's reply box. It says whose turn it is beside the leaf, such as Your turn, and Reply with the key that enters it at the line's end; a notice takes their place for its four seconds, and a walk's position follows whose turn it is.

A middle dot joins separate facts, as in In Draw mode · Thread 2 of 5, and a dash attaches a detail to the fact before it. Only words the agent wrote, such as the line it gave its work, read in its voice; what Leaf says itself, such as Stopped 3m ago, is set in italic. Words the user is writing hide the line, while screen readers still hear notices and changes of status. A press the line offers, Reply or the selected words, reads in full ink with the key that makes it, and everything else reads dimmer. The leaf and the tab's icon keep the status's colour throughout, and the leaf's menu holds the status's full sentence. A folded panel shows notices beside the leaf.

On Me

The On Me mark at the end of the panel's row counts what waits on the user: each choice a widget asks them to make, each question the agent asks them in a thread, and each update of theirs that failed to send. Pressing the mark opens the list; m walks the items in the order the list shows them, landing on each where it is answered.

The user answers an item where it asks: with the widget's own pick, or with a reply or a settling reaction, such as 👍, in its thread. Leaf adds no control of its own to end one. The tab named for the agent holds what it still owes: answers to the user's updates, and its open to-dos. Done holds each list's finished items. The On lists in How it works says exactly what belongs on each.

Notices

A notice stands in the panel's line for four seconds and is announced to screen readers. Simultaneous news shares one notice, such as Not answered · New On Me. Some news is only announced, since something on the page already shows it: an agent message the user hasn't read, which the On Me mark's dot and its margin marker show, and the user's own gesture, which the widget draws where it was made. Opening a page announces only unread agent messages, not its existing activity. The agent starting or stopping work is no notice: the status in the same line says it.

Notice When
I shortened the lede., Edited: …, 3 replies in 2 threads Announced only: an agent message the user hasn't read arrives, or one is edited. One message speaks for itself in its opening words, including anything it asks of the user.
New On Me, 2 new On Me Something new waits on the user, such as a question a reply asks or a decision a revision adds.
Not answered, Interrupted mid-answer The harness reports that a response failed or was interrupted.
Updated to v4 A new revision replaces the page in place.
Moved to Done, Undid: Moved to Done The log accepted the user's gesture, which is announced only, or its undo.

Comments and replies

Select a passage, then use the comment control or press c to write a comment. The thread keeps that passage as its anchor. Its marker opens the conversation beside the passage, where the agent's reply and the reply box appear. To comment on a whole element, such as a table or figure, ⌥-click (Alt-click) it or press s and choose it; w starts Draw mode, for drawing on the page.

The Threads tab of Leaf's On view, which the panel's On Me mark opens, lists the threads above the panel's row. Select a thread to read its messages and reply in the panel's comment field. Its passage link returns to the anchor on the page.

A reaction records brief feedback on a passage or agent reply without opening a thread. The default reactions request keeping, changing, clarifying, shortening, supporting, or prioritizing content.

What a thread shows

A thread appears in several places at once: its card in Threads, its entry in the margin, its row in the Page Map, its row in On Me, and its line in an activity feed. Each place says the same thing about it, so a thread never goes by two names or shows two statuses.

What it says Where it shows
Its name: the title, else the words of its opening message, else Drawing Threads, On Me, the margin, the Page Map, an activity feed
Its status: Your turn, On it, Still to do, Waiting, or how far the agent has got with the latest update Threads and the floating panel, as the card's status; the margin and the Page Map, beside its name
How far the agent has got with an update: Queued for Claude, Up next, Replying, Not answered, Gone quiet the message itself; the margin and Page Map entries
How many of the agent's messages the user has not read Threads, the margin, the Page Map
An unsent reply or rewrite its box, wherever the thread is open

How a thread ends

The user usually closes a thread with Resolve thread, or r on the focused thread, once they have read the answer. Its marker and ink leave the page, and it moves from Threads' Open list to Resolved. Reopen brings it back, as does z when resolving was the user's latest action.

The agent resolves a thread only when the user asks, or when no review could change the outcome; the thread then reads “Resolved by” and the agent's name. A new message from either side reopens a resolved thread. A reaction, a progress message or a failure notice does not.

z takes back the user's latest action, a message they just sent included: a comment, reply or reaction. A reaction's mark also has an undo control of its own. An undone message leaves the page, with any thread it started. Where something has already answered it, it stays in its place instead and reads Deleted. To delete an older message, the user rewrites it to no words.

Words typed but not sent are kept after their box closes. If a new version removes the content they were about, Resume writing (g i) opens them, unsent, in Comment on the page, after any words already there.

Navigation

Leaf's shortcuts act where focus is. A thread, its passage and its margin card count as one place. Text fields keep typing and caret movement; native controls keep their activation keys. Escape leaves one interaction, browser Back returns to a reading place, and Undo withdraws a recorded action.

Intent Route
Reach a control Tab / Shift + Tab; Enter activates it. A tab strip uses arrow keys.
Move through threads t advances; Shift + t goes back. Past either end, the walk comes round to the other.
Move through On Me, the items waiting on the user m advances through them; Shift + m goes back.
Choose a destination g opens hints and the Go-to map.
Find page text / opens search. Enter chooses a match; n / Shift + n then walk matches.
Link to a section The link mark beside a heading, shown under the pointer or by a tap, copies the page's address of that section. On the section, Enter copies it.
Find a thread or message With focus in Threads, / opens search. Arrow keys move through result rows; Enter opens one.
Read further j / k scroll a little; d / u move 60% of the focused reading area.
Leave or return Escape leaves one interaction. Browser Back / Forward traverses reading history. z, or Undo in the leaf's menu, takes back a recorded action.

? opens the command reference for the current context; menu rows show their own shortcuts. On touch screens, use thread titles, markers, Page Map and the leaf’s menu. The movement rules distinguish walks that wrap from lists that stop.

Phones and touch screens

Touch users can read, comment and operate widgets. Leaf’s page commands have direct gestures or visible controls; authored widgets supply their own routes. Narrow windows describes how page regions stack.

The panel and its menu

The panel fits a narrow window with space at its edges. Its row holds the leaf, a comment field showing agent status, and the On Me count beside its icon. A temporary notice replaces the status words.

The leaf opens its menu, the first tab beside On Me and Threads. It includes Search page and Page Map under Navigate, Undo, and Select mode, Draw mode, Design mode and Hide annotations under Annotations. Decisions appear in On Me and in their widgets. An active mode or search names itself in the panel and provides a ✕ exit. Search arrows appear beside the match count; tapping the marked match selects it.

Comments, threads and widgets

Select words to quote them in the panel’s field; tap the field to open a comment below the block. For a whole table, figure or other element, open the leaf’s menu → Annotations → Select mode, then tap the element: the tap opens its comment instead of pressing its controls.

Where the margin cannot hold a rail, markers appear as pins on their blocks. Tapping a pin highlights its passage and opens its thread card. Threads opens above the panel’s row. Hide annotations hides the pins.

On a touch screen, a board at most 640px wide shows a Move to row on each card, reaching offscreen columns without dragging.
