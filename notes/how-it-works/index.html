How Leaf works

A Leaf is a page an agent hands you, and the loop that carries what you do on it back to the agent. Each tab takes one part of that apart, starting with how a page is laid out.

Every page takes one of three forms

A Leaf page is a document, a sheet, or a workspace. The form belongs to the page as a whole, because it settles two things a page has exactly one of: the left edge every block starts at, and what scrolls when you scroll.

Two questions, one answer each

Lay the forms out on those two questions. Across: how wide the page is, the reading column or the window. Down: what scrolls, the page or each region of a page held to the window.

The fourth cell is empty. A page held to the window gives its height to regions set side by side, and the reading column is sized for a line of prose, not for regions beside each other. So leaf version check refuses a page whose only block is a workspace unless its main takes the window's width.

That leaves each form as one answer to both questions. A page has one left edge and one scroll, so it gives one answer, and a page cannot be two forms at once. What it can do is change form as it grows: a report that gains live status becomes a sheet, and its comments and anchors stay where they were.

A document

A document is an argument read in order: a plan, a review, a write-up, a decision. Nothing is declared. Headings, prose, tables, and diagrams all start at the reading column's edge, and the page scrolls. Most pages are documents.

A block that needs more room than the column, such as a wide diagram or a side-by-side comparison, declares data-width="wide" and is centred on the column, so the column's edge stays the page's. This page is a document, and most drawings on it are wide blocks of that kind.

<main>
  <h1>Adopt the queue-based retry</h1>
  <p class="lede">…</p>
  <section>…</section>
</main>

Examples: Review a plan, Security boundary, Release notes.

A sheet

A sheet is a set of regions read side by side: a release in progress, a board and its status, a review whose verdict and contents stay beside the diff. It takes the window's width, <main data-width="available">. Every block, the title included, starts at one left edge a gutter in from the window, and text inside a region still keeps the reading measure.

A sheet reads as one structure when its regions stand on the same vertical lines. Give it one set of tracks, usually a body beside a rail, and stack each track's regions as panels. A new grid per row puts each row's split somewhere new, and leaf version check --render advises when it finds that.

<main data-width="available">
  <h1>Release 4.2 rollout</h1>
  <lf-grid columns="2fr 1fr">
    <lf-grid columns="1">  <!-- body: panels you work through -->
    <lf-grid columns="1">  <!-- rail: status, counts, contents -->
  </lf-grid>
</main>

Examples: Live progress, Triage board, Command hub.

A workspace

A workspace is a sheet whose regions must stay in view together while each scrolls on its own: a queue beside the item it opens, a playground's controls beside its preview. Its main is a sheet's, and its only block is an lf-workspace. Each lf-pane's body scrolls under the pane's header, and actions in a pane's footer stay in reach.

It takes the window's height as well as its width, so nothing else on the page can have either. The title goes in the workspace's own header, and there is no page below the panes to scroll to.

<main data-width="available">
  <lf-workspace>
    <header><h1>Payments alerts</h1></header>
    <lf-grid columns="1fr 2fr">
      <lf-pane label="Queue">…</lf-pane>
      <lf-pane label="Alert">…</lf-pane>
    </lf-grid>
  </lf-workspace>
</main>

Examples: Notification playground, Merge a worktree.

Between the forms

Each arrangement a page might try between two forms ends up as one of the three, or is refused.

On a phone, all three are one column

The forms differ only in how they use a wide window. On a narrow one they meet: a sheet's tracks stack where the rail would fall below 16rem, a workspace flows like a sheet once the window cannot hold its regions, and every page becomes one column that the page scrolls, in the order it was written. That is why a sheet or a workspace puts its regions in an order that still reads stacked.
