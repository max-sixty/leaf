Use `lf-dial` on a page long enough that a reader might want only a quick pass, such as
a report, an investigation or a plan. At Skim the reader sees the title, the lede, the
blocks marked `.gist` and the headings above them, and nothing else. Choose those
blocks so that a reader who reads only them gets what they come to the page for: its
argument, and anything they must decide or act on.

Which passages carry that is your judgment, made for this page and its reader. Weigh:

- Who reads the page and what they come for. An incident report's reader wants the
  cause and what is owed next; a plan's reader wants what it commits to and what it
  needs from them.
- The gists read in order as one coherent account, each standing on its own without
  the text around it. A gist says what a passage concludes, not what it covers: "The
  cache key changed, so no cached cart matched" is a gist; "This section looks at the
  cache" is not.
- Early material often carries more weight, so the gists may lean toward the first
  sections, and toward the start of each, without being every section's first
  paragraph.
- A question the reader owes an answer, such as an `lf-ask`, must not be missed: mark
  it `.gist`.
- A section can contribute nothing to a quick pass, such as the method or a timeline
  the argument has already summarized. Give it no gist: Skim then folds it whole,
  heading included, behind a ⋯ the reader can press.

A heading stays at Skim only when a lede or gist follows it before the next heading of
its rank or above. The rest of each section supports its gists without restating them,
since at Read the reader sees both.

Put material only some readers want, such as method, raw output or an alternative you
ruled out, in a `details` with a summary naming it. Read leaves it closed and Everything
opens it.
