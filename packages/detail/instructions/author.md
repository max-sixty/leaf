Use `lf-dial` on a page long enough that a reader might want only a quick pass, such as
a report, an investigation or a plan. At Skim the reader sees the title, the lede, the
blocks marked `.gist` and the headings above them, and nothing else. Mark `.gist`
whatever a reader needs to understand the page at a high level: its argument, and
anything they must decide or act on. A gist may be any block. Often it is a picture
rather than words, such as a figure with its caption, a chart, a diagram, a video or a
canvas: a skimmer often reads a page by what it shows. One inside a closed `details`
stays closed.

Which blocks carry that is your judgment, made for this page and its reader. Weigh:

- Who reads the page and what they come for. An incident report's reader wants the
  cause and what is owed next; a plan's reader wants what it commits to and what it
  needs from them.
- The gists read in order as one coherent account, each standing on its own without
  the text around it. A gist in words says what a passage concludes, not what it
  covers: "The cache key changed, so no cached cart matched" is a gist; "This section
  looks at the cache" is not.
- A picture that shows the argument, such as the chart of what broke or a recording
  of what the user will see, is a gist. One that only illustrates or repeats a gist
  beside it, or decorates, is not. A gist picture's caption shows with it at Skim, so
  the caption says what the picture shows and leaves background to the text Skim
  folds.
- Early material often carries more weight, so the gists may lean toward the first
  sections, and toward the start of each, without being every section's first
  paragraph.
- A question the reader owes an answer, such as the `lf-group` holding an `lf-options`,
  must not be missed: mark it `.gist`.
- A section can contribute nothing to a quick pass, such as the method or a timeline
  the argument has already summarized. Give it no gist: a heading stays at Skim only
  when a lede or gist follows it before the next heading of its rank or above, so Skim
  folds such a section whole, heading included, behind a ⋯ the reader can press.

The rest of each section supports its gists without restating them, since at Read the
reader sees both.

Put material only some readers want, such as method, raw output or an alternative you
ruled out, in a `details` with a summary naming it. Read leaves it closed and Everything
opens it.
