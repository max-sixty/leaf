How we know the long tail is unattended

Thirty days of gateway logs. The eleven dormant integrations pin their user-agent to SDK versions from 2023 and 2024, none has fetched the migration guide, and neither brownout produced a ticket from any of them. We read them as cron jobs from the traffic shape: the same requests at the same minute past each hour, straight through the five-minute brownout.

The gateway can translate a /v1/records read onto v2 and reshape the response — renames, one date format, nothing stateful. That carries every records-only integration, and it is what makes Meridian's exemption cheap to honor: their September traffic rides the shim, not a v1 stack kept alive for one customer. /v1/search can't ride it — v2 search is cursor-paginated, and a gateway inventing page numbers over cursors would be a bug we own forever. Search leaves three integrations exposed: Ostrava, who are migrating anyway, and two from the long tail.

Nobody breaks, which is the whole argument. The cost is the emulator: it has to fake stable page numbers over a cursor API, and its edge cases are the ones that corrupt a customer's pagination silently. Two of the three integrations it would exist for are dormant.

Cutoff day, and after

  1. Freeze exemptions on 30 May. The list is Meridian, and stays Meridian.
  2. Final brownout on 2 June: 24 hours of what 30 June leaves — shimmed reads answered, everything else dark.
  3. On 30 June, flip the gateway: unshimmed v1 routes answer with the retired response below; shimmed reads carry on.
  4. Handlers stay deployed and dark for two weeks — rollback is a config revert, not a deploy.
  5. Delete the v1 handler code on 14 July.

Two changes to this section are proposed rather than made: accept or reject each in the margin, or select the words and say why not. The first comes out of the first brownout's tickets — 41 of them, mostly asking whether we were down, and a 404 would keep that conversation going.
