Feeds in Leaf

A feed card is already a Leaf item: a drafted thing to approve, edit, dismiss or discuss. Leaf lacks two things a feed needs: an agent that runs without being asked, and one list that covers every page. I recommend testing the experience first with one background session that owns a "Today" page. That needs almost no change to Leaf. If the feed earns its place, the On me list then moves across pages.

The tweet's feed brings you the next action, already drafted

Nicolas Bustamante's post argues that most people won't prompt their way into agent work, but they already know how to scroll a feed and act on what they find. So the system should bring each next useful action to them with the work done: an email already drafted, an update ready to send. The user approves, edits, dismisses, or opens a chat to go deeper. He also proposes sponsored cards to pay for the agents, which doesn't apply to Leaf.

Every verb on a feed card already exists in Leaf

Each card is one decision with its evidence beside it, which is exactly what an item framed by an lf-ask is. The On view of Leaf's panel already lists a page's items as cards, with inline answers plus Show and Discuss.

Feed card Leaf today
The work, done The card's evidence inside an lf-ask: a table, a chart, a diff
Approve An lf-options pick, a suggestion's Accept, or Approve version
Edit lf-draft: the user rewrites the text in place, and its history is recorded
Dismiss A "Not now" option, or a reject, recorded as an outcome rather than a deletion
Jump into chat Discuss on the On view card, or a comment on the exact words of the draft
The feed The On view and m, which walks On me in page order, but for one page only

The sample below is a made-up morning feed built only from today's widgets. In it, press m to walk the cards. Rewrite a word of the first draft, then pick Send. Select a phrase in the second draft and comment on it to "jump into chat". Open the On view from the panel's question mark to see the cards as a list.

What the sample can't show is what a feed needs most. Nobody prompted the agent to write these cards. Your Send has to reach an agent that may have stopped hours ago. And the cards have to come from every page and session you run, not from one page.

Leaf lacks a producer and a cross-page view, and already has the wake

  • No producer. Every Leaf page today answers a request. Nothing in Leaf starts an agent on a schedule. That belongs to the OS or the harness, not to Leaf.
  • The wake exists. A press on a page whose background job has gone idle resumes that job with claude --bg --resume (resume_claude_code_job in harness.py). Approving a card hours later therefore already reaches the agent that wrote it, on Claude Code. Codex and Pi have no equivalent.
  • No cross-page list. All pages lists each live page's agent status: Working, Awaits, No session. Each page's row publishes the agent's counts only, nothing from On me, and a page with no running server drops out of the list.

Which feed should Leaf try first?

Both options use items as the cards. They differ in where the cards come from and where you read them. They compose: a feed page from A would become one source in B's list.

Leaf code: almost noneproactive: yesrecommended A. A "Today" page owned by one background session A launchd job resumes one standing Claude Code session each morning. It checks the sources you name and writes a card for each action. Your press resumes it to carry the action out.

The work is a brief, a launchd plist and a page layout, with no change to Leaf's core. It shows whether a morning feed is worth reading before anything is built for it. The costs: one session holds every source's context, and cards from your other sessions don't reach it.

Leaf code: rows, a view, persistenceproactive: no B. On me across every page Each page's row publishes its On me items. All pages becomes one feed of every page's items. Simple items are answered inline, and the rest open their page at the item.

This addresses "many pages" (S15 in notes/what-needs-you/research.md), which you hit already with no feed at all, and any session or job that writes a page feeds it. It needs a row that outlives its server, an answer route to another page's server, and a rule for clearing stale items: in September the research counted 58 threads left waiting, median age 28 days.

A first, because the experience is the open question and A tests it cheaply

Whether a pushed feed of drafted actions is worth reading every morning is the uncertain part. A answers that in days, with nothing to undo if it isn't. B is the durable architecture whatever A finds, since it treats the feed as a view over items rather than a new kind of thing. It is worth building when either A sticks or the many-pages problem grows.

Should agents turn the offers in their replies into cards?

This is the tweet's discovery argument applied to one page. Agents end replies with offers like "say the word and I'll open the PR", and those go unanswered: the research found no agent message that put two explicit questions, while multi-part asks arrived as prose offers. An instruction could make each offer an item with Do it and Not now, so you discover what the agent could do next without inventing the prompt. It costs a change to conversation-loop.md, scored with evals/. The risk is over-asking, which the same research found destroys the signal.

Yes, try it with an eval No, leave offers in prose

Leaf's feed should drain, not scroll forever

Social feeds are tuned to keep you scrolling. Leaf's SOUL says to preserve your attention, and the research says zero should mean nothing is left to decide. So a Leaf feed should be finite. Each card expires to a recorded "not now" rather than piling up, as ChatGPT Pulse expires its morning cards after a day. An approval should carry out exactly the wording on screen when you pressed. Today nothing binds the two: the draft's edits and the option you pick are separate events, so an edit made after Send but before the agent acts leaves the approved wording unnamed. A feed needs its approval to name the text it approves. Ads are out: a card the agent didn't write for you breaks "the user directs the work".

Sources and the code each claim rests on
  • LangChain's Agent Inbox uses the same four verbs, accept, edit, respond and ignore (HumanInterruptConfig). notes/comparisons.md already lists it with HumanLayer as human-input queues.
  • server_rows.py: a row is {title, session_cwd, activity}, and activity.counts counts updates the agent owes. presence.other_leaves keeps only rows whose server is live and whose page isn't closed.
  • harness.py, ClaudeCodeHarness.nudge: the session's messaging socket first, then, for a background job with no worker, resume_claude_code_job. presence.nudge_unwatched calls it on admission, once per turn ending.
  • served_state/queues.py: on_user holds open items, to-dos on the user and resends. It is the one place the lists are assembled, and B would publish from it.
  • notes/what-needs-you/research.md: S15 "Many pages" is listed as a scenario but has no design. "A cross-page list needs reading to clear results (or page-level aging), or it starts at 58 stale items."
  • Issue #4058, "Explore independent jobs": who owns and continues a job's result is still open, and a feed of background passes is one such job.
