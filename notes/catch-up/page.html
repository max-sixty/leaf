Design · Catch up

What should a Catch up card hold?

Catch up is a deck of cards over the page, one card per item on On me, worked like Slack's: a card goes left for Later and right once it is answered, with a count, Back and an end screen. It is the swiper generalized: the deck takes any obligation as a card instead of only pass-or-keep proposals, and needs no new widget or event. What's open is what a card holds, so that every kind of item can be answered on it.

Slack's Catch up is a deck of unread conversations

On Slack's mobile app, Catch up opens a full-screen card for each conversation with unread messages, showing its newest unread messages and a count of the cards left. A swipe right marks the conversation read and moves on; a swipe left skips it and keeps it unread. Undo brings back a card swiped too quickly, holding Mark as read clears them all, and replies can be written without leaving the deck. Muted and archived channels stay out (Slack help, Slack's launch post).

Should a card hold the item itself, or a summary of it?

Both prototypes run on today's runtime over the same page of eight items, the deck a page script standing where the real one would be. Press Catch up at the bottom right, answer some cards, send some left with Later (or drag them), and finish the deck. Within the deck m is Later or Next, Shift+M is Back and Escape closes it. Each sample's Full view button gives it the whole window.

I recommend the item itself. A Leaf decision carries the evidence it is decided on, and authoring-decisions.md already asks agents to make each one answerable on its own, so the card shows exactly what the agent wrote, answered with the widget's own controls, for every kind of item including ones added later. The summary is quicker to read but leaves the evidence behind, and a draft or a question on the page still sends the user out of the deck.

The card holds the item as the agent wrote it, moved off the page while its card is in hand and put back after: the question, its evidence and its widget, live. A to-do and a thread's question, which have no place of their own on the page, show their words.

The card shows the item's title and the answers its widget declares, as today's On view does, one card at a time. A draft opens on the page; a question opens its comment box.

Every kind of item can be answered on a card

The deck reads readQueues().on_user, the list On me shows, and counts an item answered when the server's reading drops it from that list. A card that holds the item answers with the widget's own controls. A summary card uses the answers each widget declares as Decision commands (decision: true), which the On view already turns into buttons (choicesOf). Either way no widget needs code of its own for Catch up.

Should the deck also hold unread replies?

Slack's cards are unread conversations. Leaf's are agent replies in threads, and the research behind On me found they are most of what the user leaves waiting: results never acknowledged, more than questions never answered (notes/what-needs-you/research.md). A thread's words count as read once they have been on screen, through a read event, so a Keep unread would have to put them back: a new event kind and a protocol bump.

recommendedHold On me only for now Unread replies stay in Threads; decide on them after using the deck. protocol bumpAlso hold unread replies Each thread with unread words becomes a card with Reply, Resolve and Keep unread, which adds an unread event.

What building it touches

The browser alone. The deck opens from the On view and reads the On list and the walk's order (queue-walk.js); the server, the protocol and the widgets stay as they are. A card that holds the item moves the item's region with move, which keeps focus and native state, so the deck has to stand in the page's own document, as a modal dialog over it, rather than in the panel's shadow tree, where page styles wouldn't reach the item. It declares that it covers the page, so the margin's rows for items behind it are withheld; in the prototype they still show. Agents need no new instructions.
