Use `lf-deck` when the user has several independent things to get through, such as the
decisions and questions a piece of work is waiting on, and would rather take them one at
a time than scan a page of them. Each child is a card: write it as the question's named
group (`references/authoring-on-me.md`, "On the page"), holding the decision widget and
the evidence it needs, since the user sees one card at a time.
Put context that answers nothing before the deck; a card holding no question is passed
over, reached only by Back.

Where getting through the cards is the page's whole work, make the deck a workspace's
body (`references/page-layout.md`, "A workspace"), with the page's title and a line of
context in its header. The deck then fills the window below the header and each card
scrolls inside it, so the deck's bar stays put as cards of different heights come up.
Where the deck stands among other material, such as a report's findings, keep it in the
column.

The deck moves on by itself once the user answers a card, and offers Back and Later, so
don't add navigation of your own. Later leaves a card waiting and records nothing; the
deck comes back to it at the end of the pass. There the deck lists every card's
question, in the words On Me gives it, beside the answer given, so add no summary of the
answers yourself.
